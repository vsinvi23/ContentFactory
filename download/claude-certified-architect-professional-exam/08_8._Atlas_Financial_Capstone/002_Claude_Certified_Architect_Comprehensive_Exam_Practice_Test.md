During discovery, the architect learns underwriters actually spend most of their time re-requesting incomplete or illegible documents from applicants rather than reading through completed files. What should the architect do?

A.

Build the one-page summary tool as requested since it directly addresses the underwriters’ stated complaint about review time.

B.

Reframe the engagement around reducing missing-document rework, treating a summary tool as one possible component.

C.

Expand scope to build both the summary tool and a document reminder system without confirming which reduces underwriter cycle time most.

D.

Decline the request and recommend a fully autonomous agent that independently approves loan applications without underwriter review.

## Using your results[#](https://www.educative.io/courses/claude-certified-architect-professional-exam/comprehensive-assessment#Using-your-results)

A raw score on this quiz is most useful broken down by domain rather than read as one aggregate number, for exactly the reason this course has covered since its evaluation chapter. An aggregate figure can hold steady while a real weakness in one specific area hides underneath it. Count your misses within each of the seven domains above and compare that breakdown against its weight. A domain carrying nineteen percent of the real exam, like Integration, deserves a second pass through its chapter before a domain carrying seven percent does, and a domain where every miss clustered in one sub-topic points to exactly which lesson to revisit rather than the chapter as a whole.

The official exam converts a raw score to a scaled score against a passing bar of 720 out of 1000, and that conversion is not published as a simple percentage. Treat a strong result here as a genuine readiness signal, and treat a weak result in a specific domain as the most useful thing this quiz can tell you: precisely where to spend the next study session.
